1 Method: RSA in 30 seconds

Brain and LLM each produce a 14x14 distance matrix (RDM). We compare the two via Spearman rank correlation (91 upper-triangle pairs).

Brain side

14 Neurosynth meta-analytic maps (each = average of ~14,000 fMRI papers for that concept). Flatten each map, compute pairwise 1-Pearson distances -> 14x14 brain RDM.

LLM side

712 sentences across 14 conditions. Feed each as raw text (no instruction template). Extract hidden states at peak layer, mean-pool across tokens, average per condition. Pairwise 1-cosine distance -> 14x14 LLM RDM.

4 Finding 3: Interpretable Inconsistency

The alignment has a precise boundary: social-cognitive structure transfers, fine-grained affective structure does not. This supports a refined embodiment hypothesis.

4.1 Developmental trajectory (Pythia-2.8B, 9 checkpoints)

Three developmental findings:
1. Full rho monotonically rises (0.24 -> 0.72): the overall alignment strengthens during training.
2. Social structure aligns early and stays (+0.40 at step 0, stable at ~0.65).
3. Affective structure actively DIVERGES (+0.30 -> -0.57): the model develops its own emotion geometry that increasingly departs from the brain's.

4.2 Embodiment interpretation

LevelBrain-LLM aligned?Implication
Emotion vs social boundaryYESLanguage alone captures this category distinction
Social-cognitive fine structureYESChallenges strong embodiment — propositional concepts are linguistically-defined
Affective fine structureNO (diverges)Supports embodiment — body-dependent distinctions can't be learned from text
Bottom line: Embodiment is not all-or-nothing. The propositional-relational component of mentalizing is language-accessible and transfers. The body-dependent geometry of discrete emotions (organized by arousal, valence, autonomic patterns) does not. Our results draw an empirical line between what survives text compression and what does not.

4.3 Empathy: the bridge condition

13/14 conditions align at rho 0.67-0.85. Only empathy lags (rho ~ 0.24). This is consistent: empathy is a heterogeneous bridge construct (Shamay-Tsoory 2011; Zaki & Ochsner 2012) spanning affective experience-sharing (somatic) and cognitive perspective-taking (propositional). The Neurosynth map aggregates both; the LLM likely captures only the cognitive component.

6 Summary & Story

Finding 1: Consistency

4 architectures, rho ~ 0.73, scale-invariant 0.5-7B, 99.3% from pretraining. Driven by one emotion-social axis + within-social fine structure. Validated by 3 independent fMRI datasets (Narratives significant).

Finding 2: Predictability

Brain geometry predicts: block-specific coupling (4/4 DD), moral steering (rho = -0.19, p = 0.006), LLM judge confirms (brain only, 3 controls null), 2/5 prospective predictions confirmed. Developmental trajectory (Pythia): full rho monotonically rises 0.24 -> 0.72.

Finding 3: Interpretable Inconsistency

Within-affective structure does NOT transfer and actively diverges during training (+0.30 -> -0.57). Social aligns early; affective reverses. Consistent with embodiment hypothesis: propositional mentalizing transfers, body-dependent emotion geometry does not.

The story in one sentence: A text-only LLM spontaneously develops the brain's dominant affective-mentalizing axis and social-cognitive fine structure (rho ~ 0.73, 4 architectures, scale-invariant), but fine-grained affective structure actively diverges during training — drawing an empirical line between what language preserves and what requires bodily experience.
